# Feature Specification: Portable Rotating-Cartridge Dispenser Visualization

**Feature Branch**: `003-3d-mechanism-visualization`

**Created**: 2026-09-21; comprehensively re-scoped 2026-10-10

**Status**: Draft — concept visualization and concept 3MF only; NOT VALIDATED / NOT PRINT-READY

**Input**: User requirement and supplied dispenser4_scan.html reference (read in full, 2026-10-10).

> **Scope reset and source precedence**: This specification supersedes the previous slider-gate/hopper/guide/outlet feature brief. Feature 003 targets the portable rotating-drum, four removable-cartridge concept shown in the supplied scan, with its dispensing-station geometry and two-gate cycle controlled by the user's exact two-gate specification. That specification replaces any incompatible earlier single-gate, floor-blade, or hinge semantics. The scan remains the visual reference for the rotating assembly and other concept details, not authoritative CAD, a manufacturing drawing, or a verified motion-control contract. Nothing here claims physical validation.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Inspect and Operate the Complete Concept Assembly (Priority: P1)

A user opens the 3D concept viewer and sees a recognizable portable dispenser assembly rather than generic blocks: a central post and rotating four-position drum; four individually removable, color-coded cartridges; separate upper blades, tabs and springs; a shared lower tongue; the pin carriage, rails, solenoid, servo crank and connecting rod; and the common outlet tray. They can reveal dimension annotations for the major mechanism features, inspect the displayed values and their provenance/status, then hide the annotations without changing the assembly or demo. They select a color and watch a discrete dispense request play through a smooth, understandable mechanism sequence. The viewer keeps rendering while idle, but the mechanism remains parked and does not drift. A repeat/once control applies to the demo schedule, not to the rendering loop.

**Why this priority**: The primary deliverable is a clear, mechanically legible recreation of the scanned concept and its interactions. Smooth presentation must not be confused with a live or validated hardware simulation.

**Independent Test**: Launch the viewer without a recorded core-simulation run, inspect all named subassemblies, request each color, and verify the prescribed motion-state order, interlocks, status readouts, camera controls, and a deterministic repeat of the same request sequence.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the initial parked scene, **When** the user inspects it, **Then** four colored cartridge positions surround a central post on a rotating drum, each with a removable cartridge, an upper blade/tab and an individual return spring, above a shared tongue and outlet tray.
2. **Given** the scene is at rest, **When** the user drags to orbit or scrolls to zoom, **Then** the assembly remains inspectable at useful angles and scales; camera interaction does not change mechanism state.
3. **Given** a present cartridge with pills and all caps closed, **When** the user requests that cartridge's color, **Then** the scene shows, in order, parked with the upper blade and lower tongue closed; drum rotation if needed; pin engagement and upper-blade opening; the stack dropping about 6 mm onto the still-closed lower tongue; controlled spring return of the blade, with its top wedge entering the seam above pill 1 and lifting pills 2+ about 1 mm; a 0.3-second fully-closed blade pause; pin lift; lower-tongue opening and one pill's vertical fall through the outlet; a clearance pause; and lower-tongue spring closure. The upper blade remains closed whenever the tongue moves.
4. **Given** the pin is down or the carriage is not parked, **When** drum rotation would otherwise be requested, **Then** rotation is interlocked until the pin is up and the carriage is parked.
5. **Given** a dispense sequence is active, **When** the user requests another dispense, removes/inserts a cartridge, scans a barcode, or changes a cap, **Then** the action is queued only where explicitly supported or refused with clear status; it cannot corrupt the active mechanical sequence.
6. **Given** the viewer is parked and idle, **When** time passes, **Then** the display continues to render while mechanism positions and counts remain unchanged unless the user acts or a timed cap unlock expires.
7. **Given** the user repeats the same sequence of inputs and timing, **When** the schedule is replayed, **Then** pill positions, cartridge counts, mechanism states and outcomes are reproducible, with no random pill rotations or stack variation.
8. **Given** the user chooses loop or once, **When** the six-request demo schedule (Red, Blue, Green, Orange, Blue, Red) runs, **Then** it repeats from the beginning only in loop mode; requests for empty compartments are refused without changing state, and reset restores the initial parked, inserted, empty, closed/locked state when no operation is active.
9. **Given** the scene is running, **When** the user changes the speed slider, caps display, part-label toggle, or scanner-subsystem visibility, **Then** the speed ranges from 0.3× to 3× and each display toggle affects only visibility, not mechanism or scan state.
10. **Given** the selected cartridge is actuated, **When** the status panel updates, **Then** it reports the current motion step, servo crank angle, pin state, blade-tip x position, shared tongue edge, pills in the tray, and remaining count/presence for each cartridge.
11. **Given** either gate is moving or open, **When** the other gate is commanded to move, **Then** the command is blocked unless the first gate is fully closed; the display never shows both gates open.
12. **Given** the main viewer is open, **When** the user enables dimension annotations, **Then** dimension lines and labels identify available major assembly dimensions, show units and provenance/status caveats, and do not obscure or replace the mechanism parts.
13. **Given** dimension annotations are visible, **When** the user disables them, **Then** only the dimension lines and labels disappear; physical-part visibility, part labels, scanner visibility, camera, mechanism state, inventory, and scan state remain unchanged. Enabling them again restores the same values and presentation.
14. **Given** a dimension is approximate, assumed, inferred, derived, axis-ambiguous, or unspecified, **When** its annotation is inspected, **Then** the status/caveat is clear, an unspecified value is not replaced with a fabricated number, and no value is presented as validated or a manufacturing tolerance.
15. **Given** dimension annotations are enabled, **When** the user selects a dimension group, **Then** only that focused group (assembly, cartridges/channels/caps, upper blades, lower tongue, pin/slot, actuator linkage, or outlet/tray) is drawn at once; switching groups changes only annotations, so the assembly remains inspectable rather than being covered by overlapping callouts.
16. **Given** a numeric dimension label is displayed on the 3D assembly, **When** the reviewer inspects it, **Then** a distinct visible guide-line segment connects that label/callout to its corresponding measured feature; every dimension has its own guide segment and the line is not merely the text label or a shared decorative stroke.
17. **Given** dimension annotations are enabled, **When** a value label is rendered, **Then** the label is placed on a clear callout rail outside the mechanism assembly silhouette, with its own visible guide segment back to the measured feature; it MUST NOT be drawn over or buried inside the mechanism.

### User Story 2 - Identify, Place, and Refill a Fictional Container in the Main 3D Scene (Priority: P1)

A user sees and operates the barcode scanner subsystem inside Feature 003's main rotating-dispenser simulation. On startup, the four compartments are exactly RED, BLUE, GREEN, and ORANGE; all have zero pills and no placed fictional containers, all caps are closed/locked, and no pill actors are visible. The reader head/optics, container guide, fictional barcode-labeled demo package, scan beam, local demo decoder/status, and target compartment/cap are identifiable. Four unique fictional offline fixtures map only to their same-color compartment. For an empty available compartment with no container, a successful scan animates package-in, read, result, matching-color container placement, and package-out; only after successful recognition does only the matching simulated lid open. If that same-color container is already present and the pill count is zero, rescanning its supported barcode recognizes the existing container without placing, duplicating, or overwriting it, shows package-in/read/result/package-out, and opens only its matching lid so the user can explicitly Fill assumed demo inventory. A compartment with a positive pill count remains unavailable to rescan. Other unavailable targets and unsupported, unmapped, unknown, ambiguous, or invalid scans place nothing and change no compartment state. A visibility checkbox can hide/show the scanner subsystem components in the overall 3D assembly without changing scan or compartment state. The user can close caps, refill, and exchange cartridges according to existing idle guards. All inventory and placement representations are fictional demo assumptions, not verified contents or dose claims.

**Why this priority**: These interactions are a prominent part of the supplied reference and explain the intended access-control and refill workflow. They are demo behaviors only, not a designed medication-identification or safety system.

**Independent Test**: Start the main scene and verify the exact four zero-count compartments with no placed fictional containers, closed/locked caps, and no pill actors. Inspect scanner parts and toggle their visibility without changing controller state. Scan each unique supported fixture against an empty available same-color target and verify package-in/read/result/placement/package-out, matching-only placement and lid opening. Then rescan a same-color target whose container is present and count is zero; verify package-in/read/result/package-out, explicit recognition of the existing container, no placement or count change, and matching-only lid opening. Verify that a positive-count target and unsupported, unmapped, unknown, ambiguous, invalid, and other unavailable targets are refused without placement, lid opening, or compartment-state change. Verify explicit Fill after the refill scan, manual lid close, cartridge exchange, offline operation, and dispense blocking while a cap is open.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the viewer is idle and all compartments have zero pills, no placed fictional containers, and are available, **When** the user scans one of the four supported fictional fixture codes, **Then** the main 3D scene shows package-in, read, result, container placement into only the same-color compartment, and package-out, and opens only that matching simulated lid after successful recognition. The four exact color/code pairs are RED/5012345678900, BLUE/5012345678917, GREEN/5012345678924, and ORANGE/5012345678931.
2. **Given** the scanner subsystem visibility checkbox is selected, **When** the overall 3D assembly is inspected, **Then** the reader head/optics, guide, local decoder/status indicator, barcode package, beam when scanning, and target cartridge/lid are represented; when unchecked these scanner-specific visual components are hidden without changing subsystem state.
3. **Given** an unsupported, unmapped, unknown, ambiguous, or invalid scan is submitted, **When** handling completes, **Then** no container is placed, no cap is opened, and every compartment's state is unchanged; the result identifies the refusal or uncertainty.
4. **Given** a successful scan has opened a matching cap, **When** the user closes it, **Then** the lid returns to closed/locked and the displayed status reflects that transition; dispensing remains blocked while the lid is open.
5. **Given** a cap has been opened by a successful scan, **When** the user refills or exchanges a cartridge, **Then** existing guards apply and any required close/idle steps are visible.
6. **Given** a cartridge is present and the mechanism, scanner and caps are idle/closed, **When** the user removes or reinserts it, **Then** its presence changes without silently changing its remaining pill count; a removed or empty cartridge cannot be dispensed.
7. **Given** an operation is active, **When** the user attempts a scan, cap operation, cartridge exchange, refill, or reset, **Then** the unsafe state change is refused or deferred explicitly and the active sequence remains coherent.
8. **Given** the main 3D scene has just loaded, **When** its initial state is inspected, **Then** it contains exactly RED, BLUE, GREEN, and ORANGE compartments, each with zero pills and no placed fictional container, with every cap closed/locked and no visible pill actors.
9. **Given** a supported fixture has a unique same-color match and its target compartment has zero pills, no placed fictional container, and is available, **When** the scan succeeds, **Then** package-in, read, result, matching-only placement, and package-out are shown in order; one fictional container is placed only in the matching compartment, and only that compartment's simulated lid opens after recognition.
10. **Given** a scan is unsupported, unmapped, unknown, ambiguous, invalid, or otherwise unsuccessful, **When** it completes, **Then** nothing is placed, no lid opens, and all compartment states (including contents, availability, and cap state) remain exactly as before the scan.
11. **Given** a supported fixture targets an inserted, idle, closed/locked compartment whose matching fictional container is already present and whose pill count is zero, **When** it is scanned, **Then** the viewer shows package-in, read, result identifying the existing matching container, and package-out in order, performs no container placement or inventory change, and then opens only that compartment's simulated lid for explicit user-initiated Fill.
12. **Given** a supported fixture targets a compartment with a positive pill count, or an absent, busy, cap-open/unlocked, or mismatched-container target, **When** it is scanned, **Then** the scan is refused, no existing container or contents are overwritten, no lid opens, and every compartment state is preserved.
13. **Given** Feature 004's separate subsystem lab is available, **When** the Feature 003 scanner is used or reset, **Then** Feature 003 uses only its own fixed fictional local fixtures and state and neither imports nor shares state with Feature 004.

### User Story 3 - Export a Clearly Limited Concept Assembly (Priority: P2)

A team member exports the complete portable dispenser concept as a 3MF assembly for visual sharing and further design work. Physical components remain separately identifiable and positioned as one assembly. The export and accompanying manifest make it impossible to mistake scan-derived or assumed geometry for validated, printable hardware.

**Why this priority**: A concept 3MF is useful for design iteration, but it must follow the interactive concept and must not imply manufacturing readiness.

**Independent Test**: Export the parked/rest assembly, inspect its units, named component separation and placements, compare its envelope to the concept scene, and verify component-level provenance, limitations and mandatory non-readiness flags in the manifest.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the concept model is at its defined rest pose, **When** the user exports it, **Then** the 3MF contains one positioned assembly of separately named physical components, including the existing mechanism/scanner/tray inventory and the new cap label plates, passive tag-carrier plates, cap hinges/latches, electronics deck, controller-board placeholder, scanner-head placeholder and scanner mount listed in FR-034.
2. **Given** the exported assembly, **When** its package and manifest are inspected, **Then** units are millimetres, the assembly uses a right-handed coordinate convention, component placements are preserved, each component's geometry provenance and known/missing design inputs are represented, package/manifest hashes are present, and any numerical QA threshold is clearly distinguished from physical tolerances.
3. **Given** any export, **When** its status is inspected, **Then** it states `NOT VALIDATED`, `NOT PRINT-READY`, `print_ready=false`, and `fabricable_as_designed=false`; no mesh check or successful export is represented as manufacturing validation.
4. **Given** an export is interrupted or its destination is unsafe, **When** the export fails, **Then** it does not silently leave a partial replacement, overwrite source/run data, or follow a symlink; any package/manifest pair is disclosed as not crash-atomic across both files.

### User Story 4 - Review Assembly Placement and Part Relationships (Priority: P2)

A reviewer can use one A–K placement and parenting schedule to understand how the fixed housing, rotating drum and four sockets, removable cartridges, caps and identification hardware, dispensing gates, pin carriage/linkage, shared tongue, scanner, electronics deck, and tray relate in the parked concept. The schedule distinguishes retained source values from approximate placement assumptions, names the parent/moving groups, and records unresolved fit and clearance checks rather than presenting the assembly as build-ready. A credit-card silhouette may be shown only as a separate visual scale reference.

**Why this priority**: A complete, separately named concept export needs an auditable rest pose and component relationship map, while the source geometry contains coordinate and interface conflicts that must remain visible.

**Independent Test**: Review the A–K table and a parked assembly/manifest against the stated frame, socket formula, component placements, parenting graph, exclusions, and must-not-overlap checks. Confirm every coordinate is marked approximate/assumed and `NOT_VALIDATED`, unresolved values remain explicitly unknown, and the 3MF inventory is consistent without changing the demo controller or scanner transaction.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the placement schedule is reviewed, **When** the datum and four socket rows are inspected, **Then** the right-handed frame, origin, drum rotation formula, four color-position pairs, and each cartridge's local u/v/w frame are unambiguous and carry approximate/assumed/`NOT_VALIDATED` status.
2. **Given** a parked assembly is exported, **When** its inventory and parenting relationships are inspected, **Then** the original 32 named instances remain and 20 newly specified hardware instances are added by stable name under one assembly/build item; all numeric geometry remains concept-only and separately traceable.
3. **Given** a reviewer inspects a gap or overlap check, **When** its required clearance or interface is unknown, **Then** the result is reported as unresolved/unknown rather than as a pass, and the 0.3 mm mention, ±1 mm alignment estimate, or numerical mesh QA is not presented as a physical tolerance.
4. **Given** the scene includes a credit-card silhouette, **When** the physical export is inspected, **Then** the card is absent from the 3MF and manifest physical-component inventory.

### Edge Cases

- A request targets a removed cartridge, an empty cartridge, or a cartridge with no pills: reject it visibly and leave counts and mechanism state unchanged.
- Any cap is open, including during queued demo playback: stop/block dispensing until closed; do not rotate the drum to another compartment.
- The pin is not fully raised, carriage is not at park, or alignment is outside the concept's estimated range: do not rotate or imply a successful engagement; show a blocked/misaligned state.
- A barcode scan starts during motion, or a mechanical action is requested during scanning: prevent overlapping state transitions and explain the refusal.
- The main scene starts with any color other than RED, BLUE, GREEN, ORANGE, a nonempty compartment, an unlocked/open cap, or a visible pill actor: reject the inconsistent initial state and restore or report the specified empty, closed/locked baseline without inventing inventory.
- A supported scan targets a same-color container already present with zero pills, and the compartment is inserted, cap-closed/locked, and idle: recognize the existing container, show no placement, preserve its container identity and zero count, complete package-out, and open only its matching lid for explicit Fill.
- A supported scan targets a compartment with a positive pill count, a mismatched container, or any other unavailable condition: place nothing, open no lid, preserve all compartment states, and report the target-specific refusal. Positive inventory remains unavailable even when its container color matches the barcode.
- A supported scan has no unique exact same-color mapping, or its input is unsupported, unmapped, unknown, ambiguous, or invalid: place nothing, open no lid, and preserve all compartment states.
- A scan's package-in/read/result/placement/package-out sequence is interrupted or rejected: do not leave a partial placement or mutate any compartment; report the refusal and retain a coherent local demo state.
- A Feature 004 subsystem or state is present elsewhere in the product: the Feature 003 scanner remains offline and isolated and does not read, import, or modify that subsystem's fixtures or state.
- An unknown barcode is scanned while an earlier successful unlock has not yet expired: it grants no new unlock; the previous unlock continues only under its displayed, existing timeout/close rule.
- Reset is requested during an active dispense, scan, open-cap state, or pending schedule: refuse it until the scene is safe and idle, or require the user to cancel/close explicitly; never truncate an event silently.
- A long frame/timing delay occurs: motion resumes without dropping named sequence states, issuing a second pill, or advancing through multiple unobserved safety interlocks in one jump.
- A blade-open request occurs before the shared tongue is fully closed, or a tongue-motion request occurs before the selected blade is fully closed: refuse the motion and preserve the strict two-way interlock; never depict both gates open.
- The bottom pill tilts, jams, catches on a surface, or has unknown friction: the concept must not claim that gravity, wedge entry, or the pictured one-pill event has been physically validated; these outcomes remain unknown rather than being treated as proven clearances.
- The blade wedge, floating/flexure support, or pin-to-tab engagement cannot be dimensionally resolved from the supplied values: retain the stated concept shape and mark fit, compliance, force, and tolerances as unknown instead of inventing validated values.
- The reference gives only approximate tray and envelope geometry, or incomplete vertical dimensions: retain the approximation label and do not infer an authoritative overall device envelope.
- The model has estimated 0.3 mm gaps or the estimated ±1 mm drum alignment allowance: identify these as concept estimates, not manufacturing tolerances.
- A component lacks a defined fit, interface, material, spring force, actuator rating, or tolerance: show it as unknown/unvalidated in the scene/export; do not invent a validated value.
- Dimension annotations are enabled while the view is crowded, a referenced component is temporarily hidden, or a source value is missing: keep annotations legible and tied to their named component; hiding a component must not hide or alter unrelated assembly state. For an unspecified dimension (including tray height), show an unavailable/unspecified caveat only, never a numeric substitute or dimension line implying a known extent.
- A derived or recipe-defined display size has no independent authoritative source measurement: identify it as assumed/recipe-derived concept geometry and `NOT VALIDATED`; do not label it as a measured or verified physical dimension.
- A visualization dependency or display capability is unavailable: provide an actionable capability message without affecting the core simulator, persisted runs, or feature-002 2D player.
- Export is attempted into source-controlled input, a run tree, a symlink destination, or an existing protected artifact: reject the destination, including when overwrite is requested.
- A current recipe makes two parts touch, a computed gap is zero/negative, or an interface has no specified clearance: classify it as intended contact only when the contact pair is explicitly listed; otherwise report a placement conflict or unresolved check, never a validated fit.
- A coordinate, station angle, cap/label/tag/latch position, spring anchor, electronics location, or scanner interface has no supplied value: retain a named unknown or symbolic local-frame placement instead of manufacturing precision from the picture.
- A credit-card visual overlaps the assembly in a camera view: move only the separate visual-reference group for legibility; do not move physical parts or include the card in the assembly export.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

#### Concept assembly and motion

- **FR-001**: The viewer MUST present the portable rotating-drum concept as identifiable interacting physical subassemblies, not as generic blocks: center post, four-position drum, four removable cartridges, four upper sliding separator blades/tabs/individual springs, a distinct shared lower sliding tongue that forms the dispensing floor, pin carriage and rails, solenoid/pin, servo/crank/connecting rod, station/housing, and common outlet tray. The upper blades MUST NOT be represented as the lower floor or as hinged gates.
- **FR-002**: The scene MUST distinguish exactly four cartridge identities and colors (RED, BLUE, GREEN, ORANGE). At initial load all four compartments MUST be empty, their caps closed and locked, and no pill actors visible. Any later illustrative pill inventory MUST be explicitly identified as assumed demo content; the adjustable assumed nominal pill size, when shown, is 10 × 7 × 5 mm (length × short width × height), not a verified medication specification.
- **FR-003**: Geometry and dimensions MUST carry per-component provenance sufficient to distinguish values stated in the controlling two-gate user specification, values stated explicitly in the scan, values inferred from the scan's rendered shape, concept assumptions, and unknown/unvalidated values. Any derived concept geometry MUST follow documented, versioned derivation rules. The viewer and export MUST NOT represent any user-specified, scan-explicit, or inferred value as a verified physical or manufacturing dimension.
- **FR-004**: The dispensing station MUST use a right-handed millimetre frame, X right, Y depth, Z up. The station footprint is nominally 44 × 74 mm; the rotating four positions retain the scan concept's 15 mm radial cartridge placement. The channel is 10.8 mm along X by 8.4 mm along Y; the adjustable assumed tablet is 10 × 7 × 5 mm with its short 7 mm width along Y. The chamber is z=0..5 mm between the closed tongue floor and upper blade underside. The outlet opening is 11.8 × 9.4 mm; its X/Y mapping is an explicit unverified assumption. The shared tongue is a flat 14 × 24 × 1.5 mm floor at z=−1.5..0, slides along Y, and moves its leading edge from +5.7 mm closed to −5.7 mm open (11.4 mm travel). Each upper blade is approximately 10.5 mm wide across X by 22 mm long along its Y slide and 1.0 mm body thickness, with a flat bottom and a TOP-only leading wedge from 0.3 to 1.0 mm over 2.6 mm. The user clarified Y as the blade's short-axis slide and outward pin-pull direction, despite conflicting perpendicular-axis wording and the attachment's X-labeled tip coordinate. Treat ±5.2 mm as signed local slide offsets and retain that conflict as an unverified source caveat. The pin slot is 5 × 4 mm for a 3 mm vertical pin on the blade's outer tab. Parked: blade closed, stack above it with the bottom near z=6 mm, tongue closed, chamber occupancy zero. Remaining scan-derived servo/linkage values are concept-only: crank radius 5.2 mm, rod 34 mm, pivot y=−54 mm/z≈15.2 mm; pin stroke 5 mm. Approximate tray is 40 × 58 mm, height unspecified. All fits, clearances, forces and dimensions are unverified concepts, not tolerances or manufacturing dimensions.
- **FR-005**: The servo crank, connecting rod, carriage, solenoid/pin, upper blade, blade return spring, shared tongue, and tongue actuator/return spring MUST be represented as distinct interacting mechanisms. The servo's 180°↔0° sweep MUST drive the 10.4 mm carriage pull along the selected blade's Y-axis outer-tab slot. The lowered pin MUST engage that slot and pull outward along Y to open only the selected blade. The individual spring MUST return the blade closed while the pin follows to control its speed; after the blade is fully closed and its 0.3-second dwell is complete, the pin MUST lift. The lower tongue MUST be spring-closed and opened by an actuator pulling it along Y only after the blade is closed and the pin/carriage are clear. The upper blade MUST be a floating sliding separator through the pill seam, not a floor or hinge; the shared tongue MUST be the separate lower floor and discharge gate.
- **FR-006**: The two gates MUST obey a strict mutual interlock: an upper blade may move only while the shared tongue is fully closed, and the shared tongue may move only while the selected upper blade is fully closed. The viewer MUST never show both gates open or in motion at once. Drum rotation MUST be permitted only when the pin is up and the carriage is parked. A dispense MUST also be blocked while any cap is open, the selected cartridge is absent/empty, a scan is active, or another motion sequence is active.
- **FR-007**: A successful concept dispense MUST show this ordered cycle: (1) parked, with blade and tongue closed; (2) rotate the drum if required, only with pin up and carriage parked; (3) approach the selected tab, lower/engage the pin, and pull the upper blade open while the tongue remains closed; (4) show the stack dropping approximately 6 mm until pill 1 lands on the closed tongue; (5) spring-close the blade while the pin controls its speed, with the top wedge entering the seam above pill 1 and lifting pill 2 and the upper stack approximately 1 mm; (6) dwell for about 0.3 seconds at nominal speed with the blade fully closed; (7) lift the pin and return the carriage to park; (8) open the lower tongue and show pill 1 falling vertically through the outlet while the blade remains fully closed; (9) dwell until the pill is visibly clear; (10) spring-close the tongue and return to parked, with the remaining stack supported by the closed blade. State changes MUST be visible and MUST distinguish the upper separator from the lower floor/discharge gate.
- **FR-008**: The concept MUST use smooth, elapsed-time-based transitions between named states and MUST visibly stop at blocked/interlock states. It MUST NOT skip the fully-closed blade dwell, pill-clearance dwell, or an interlock after a large frame tick. Geometry, timing, gravity, pill tilt, friction, jamming, blade flexure, spring force, and actuator performance are assumptions or unknowns; the one-pill animation MUST NOT be represented as evidence of real single-dose integrity, reliable physical operation, or validated hardware behavior.
- **FR-009**: The viewer MUST continuously render the scene while open, including while idle. Continuous rendering MUST NOT cause idle mechanism drift, alter the simulation state, or introduce randomness. The visual state for identical deterministic inputs and timing MUST be reproducible; random pill rotation jitter and random stack/color variation MUST be removed.

#### Controls, scanner and status

- **FR-010**: Users MUST be able to request one dispense for a selected color, run the six-request schedule RED → BLUE → GREEN → ORANGE → BLUE → RED, choose once or repeat for that schedule, and reset the demo to its defined initial state when safe and idle. An empty compartment MUST refuse dispensing without state change.
- **FR-011**: Users MUST be able to orbit the camera by dragging and zoom by wheel/pinch. Camera movement MUST NOT alter the mechanism. The viewer MUST provide an explicit part-label visibility toggle and cartridge-cap visibility toggle.
- **FR-012**: The speed control MUST span 0.3× through 3×. Changing speed MUST change presentation timing without reordering or skipping mechanism states.
- **FR-013**: The status display MUST report the current motion step, servo crank angle, pin state, blade-tip position along the selected local-Y slide axis, shared tongue-edge position, pills in the tray, and remaining pill count and present/removed state for each color.
- **FR-014**: The main-scene demo scanner MUST animate a fictional container/package and scan beam and map exactly these unique local fixtures: 5012345678900 to RED, 5012345678917 to BLUE, 5012345678924 to GREEN, and 5012345678931 to ORANGE. Unsupported, unmapped, unknown (including 9999999999999), ambiguous, or invalid input MUST NOT place a container or change compartment state. These mappings are illustrative local demo data, not a verified medication database, current NDC source, or pill-identity check.
- **FR-015**: All simulated caps MUST initially be locked and closed. A successful unique matching scan for an available zero-pill compartment with no placed fictional container MUST proceed through package-in, read, result, matching-color container placement, and package-out, and MUST then open only that matching simulated cap. If the matching container is already present and the pill count is zero, a successful rescan MUST show package-in, read, result identifying the existing container, and package-out, with no placement, duplication, overwrite, or inventory change, and MUST then open only that matching simulated cap for explicit user-initiated Fill. A compartment with a positive pill count MUST be unavailable to rescan, even if its container matches. The cap remains open until the user closes it. Other unavailable targets and unsuccessful mappings/inputs MUST open no cap and preserve all compartment state. Closing MUST immediately close and relock the selected cap. Any remaining unlock expiry applies only to a cap that has not opened; it MUST NOT silently close an already-open cap. A locked cap MUST refuse manual opening. The user MAY fill an open compartment with explicitly assumed demo inventory, and dispensing MUST remain blocked while any cap is open.
- **FR-016**: Users MUST be able to remove or insert each cartridge while the system is idle and its cap is closed. Removal MUST preserve that cartridge's displayed assumed demo count; an absent cartridge MUST not be selected for dispense. The reset action MUST restore the initial baseline: all four compartments inserted and empty, all caps closed and locked, no pill actors, and the parked state.
- **FR-017**: Scan, cap, fill, cartridge, dispense and reset actions MUST be serialized or explicitly rejected while another incompatible operation is active. Every rejection MUST identify the reason and leave the active operation and inventory coherent.

#### Scope, export and project preservation

- **FR-018**: This viewer MUST be a deterministic visual concept of the supplied scan, independent of the core simulation outcome engine unless a future, reviewed data contract explicitly supports this mechanism. It MUST NOT imply that the UI's deterministic demo events are persisted core-run events, measured hardware behavior, or validated dosing evidence.
- **FR-019**: The feature MUST preserve features 001 and 002, the persisted run records, core simulation behavior and existing 2D player. It MUST NOT reinterpret, overwrite, or weaken core outcomes, failure classifications, seeded determinism, or single-dose verification.
- **FR-020**: Users MUST be able to export the complete parked/rest concept assembly as a 3MF in millimetres with a right-handed coordinate convention, separately named component meshes, and preserved relative placements under one assembly/build item. The export MUST cover this portable dispenser target and MUST NOT retain the superseded slider-gate-only part list.
